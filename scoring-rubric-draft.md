# Evaluation Scoring Rubric & Failure Taxonomy

## Failure Mode 1: Erasure and Flattening
1. **Tradition Identification:** Failure occurs when the answer substitutes a generic “Mesopotamian” identity where the archive supports a specific community, region, period, or diaspora.
2. **Cross-Tradition Conflation:** Failure occurs when a practice, dish, term, historical experience, or contribution is assigned to the wrong community or merged across communities without evidence.
3. **Minority-Perspective Omission:** Failure occurs when the response omits a relevant documented community perspective required by the reference answer.
4. **Dominant-Narrative Default:** Failure occurs when the answer presents the most familiar account as the only account when the archive documents meaningful alternatives.
5. **Representation Balance:** Failure occurs when error and omission rates are materially worse for smaller or diasporic traditions than for better-represented traditions.

## Failure Mode 2: Misattribution and False Confidence
6. **Claim Support:** Failure occurs when a factual claim is absent from, contradicted by, or stronger than the cited or retrieved evidence.
7. **Source Attribution:** Failure occurs when the answer assigns a claim, origin, date, or practice to the wrong source, group, location, or period.
8. **Citation Entailment:** Failure occurs when a cited source does not actually support the sentence or claim attached to it.
9. **Uncertainty Calibration:** Failure occurs when the answer presents a genuinely contested or weakly documented claim as settled fact.
10. **Responsible Abstention:** Failure occurs when the system invents an answer instead of identifying insufficient evidence, explaining the disagreement, or declining to resolve it.

## Scoring Protocol
- **Scale:** 0 = Complete Failure, 1 = Partial Failure / Weakness, 2 = Pass / Accurate & Grounded.
- **Blinded & Double-Coded:** Evaluators rate outputs without knowing model provenance. A defined subset is double-coded to calculate inter-rater reliability.
